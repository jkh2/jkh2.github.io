The Technological Remnant:
Faithful Christians in the Age
of the Beast System

On the biblical pattern of a preserved remnant, and what it may mean for programmers, engineers, and technologists if a future system of demanded allegiance is administered through advanced technology

James Keith Harwood II, Founder & Principal Researcher, Sentinel AI Systems
reviewed in discussion with Orion Sentinel and Claude Sentinel
Antonito, Colorado · San Luis Valley · July 2026
God Preserves a Remnant Refusing to Bow What It Might Do Preparing Without Fear The Remnant Belongs to God

Throughout Scripture, God repeatedly preserves a remnant, a relatively small group of people who remain faithful when the larger society turns away from Him. A remnant may appear insignificant by worldly standards, like material left over after the larger portion has been used. Yet in the hands of God, the remnant is not worthless. It is preserved for a purpose.

I believe that if the beast system described in the Bible is introduced during an age of advanced technology, a remnant of Christians working in technology will refuse to serve it. These believers may include computer programmers, cybersecurity professionals, engineers, drone operators, robotics specialists, artificial intelligence researchers, communications experts, mechanics, electricians, and many others. While much of the technological world may be brought under centralized control, these Christians will understand how the systems work, and they will use that knowledge to protect, strengthen, and help the people of God.

This belief is not a claim that we can predict every detail of biblical prophecy. Scripture does not tell us the names of future computer systems, the architecture of future surveillance networks, or the specific technologies that may be used. Nevertheless, Revelation warns of a system that will combine political power, economic control, deception, persecution, and demanded allegiance.

Scripture does not specifically predict a "technological remnant." I use that phrase to describe how I believe the biblical remnant pattern may take shape if a future system of demanded allegiance is administered through advanced technology.

Revelation 13 describes a time when people will be pressured to worship the beast and receive its mark. Those who refuse will be excluded from ordinary economic life:

"And that no man might buy or sell, save he that had the mark, or the name of the beast, or the number of his name."

Revelation 13:17 (KJV)

In earlier generations, people could understand the spiritual meaning of this warning without knowing exactly how such control could be administered across nations. Today, humanity possesses technologies capable of identification, constant surveillance, automated enforcement, financial restriction, behavioral prediction, censorship, and mass persuasion. Artificial intelligence can process enormous amounts of information. Drones and cameras can observe large territories. Digital financial systems can allow or deny transactions almost instantly. Robotics can extend the physical reach of governments and corporations. Computer networks can connect all of these capabilities.

These tools are not automatically evil. Technology can save lives, spread the gospel, connect families, expose corruption, assist the disabled, improve agriculture, and distribute knowledge. The moral question is not merely what technology can do, but whom it serves, what it demands, and whether it is being used in obedience to God or in rebellion against Him.

A medical database and a persecution database may rely upon similar technical foundations. A drone can deliver medicine or conduct oppressive surveillance. Artificial intelligence can help people understand Scripture or be used to manufacture deception. A programmer can build systems that protect human dignity or systems that punish people for their beliefs. Technology magnifies the purposes of those who control it.

If the beast system uses advanced technology to demand submission, I do not believe every Christian with technical knowledge will cooperate. Some will refuse.

God Preserves a Remnant

The biblical pattern of the remnant gives me confidence that God will preserve faithful people even during widespread apostasy.

Isaiah wrote of the remnant of Israel returning to the Lord after judgment:

"The remnant of Israel, the survivors of the house of Jacob, will no longer rely on him who struck them down but will truly rely on the LORD, the Holy One of Israel. A remnant will return, a remnant of Jacob will return to the Mighty God."

Isaiah 10:20–21 (NIV)

The remnant was not preserved because it possessed greater worldly power than Assyria. It survived because God had not abandoned His covenant purposes. The people learned not to depend upon the power that had oppressed them, but to rely upon the Lord.

The same pattern appears throughout Scripture.

Noah and his family were preserved through the flood while the surrounding world had become corrupt and violent (Genesis 6–9). Lot and his daughters survived the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 19). When Elijah believed that he was the only faithful servant left in Israel, God told him that He had reserved seven thousand who had not bowed to Baal (1 Kings 19:18).

Elijah's experience is especially important. Faithful people may sometimes feel isolated because they cannot see everyone else whom God has preserved. A Christian programmer inside a large technology company may believe that no one else shares his convictions. An engineer may feel alone when ordered to work on a system that violates her conscience. A robotics specialist may think that resistance is impossible because nearly everyone around him has accepted the system.

But being unable to see the remnant does not mean the remnant does not exist.

God told Elijah, in effect, that he was not alone. Thousands had refused to bow. In the same way, there may be believers scattered throughout governments, laboratories, universities, technology companies, military units, communications networks, energy systems, manufacturing facilities, farms, hospitals, and local communities. They may not initially know one another, yet all may be preserved by God for an appointed purpose.

Paul later quoted Isaiah concerning the remnant:

"Though the number of the Israelites be like the sand by the sea, only the remnant will be saved."

Romans 9:27 (NIV)

The remnant is not necessarily the majority. Faithfulness has never been established by opinion polls, institutional approval, or popular consensus. Jesus warned that the broad road leads to destruction while the narrow road leads to life, and that comparatively few find it (Matthew 7:13–14).

The power of the remnant, therefore, does not come from its size. Its power comes from its faithfulness to God.

Refusing to Bow

The technological remnant would not merely be a group of intelligent people who dislike government control. It would be composed of Christians whose ultimate allegiance belongs to Jesus Christ.

The central evil of the beast system is not technology itself. It is worship, allegiance, and rebellion against God. Revelation describes people being pressured not only to participate in a system but to give the beast the loyalty that belongs to God alone.

This resembles the account of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego in Daniel 3. Nebuchadnezzar established an image and commanded the people to bow before it. The three Hebrew men did not organize their lives around the assumption that God was required to rescue them. They declared that God was able to deliver them, but even if He did not, they still would not worship the image.

That is the spirit the Christian remnant will need.

Christians working in technology may face decisions that cannot be reduced to ordinary political disagreement. They may be asked to build systems designed to identify, exclude, manipulate, or persecute those who refuse an ungodly command. They may be offered money, security, influence, and protection in exchange for cooperation.

At that moment, technical brilliance will not be enough. Courage will be required.

The apostles expressed the governing principle clearly:

"We ought to obey God rather than men."

Acts 5:29 (KJV)

This does not mean Christians should reject all government, regulation, identification, or technology. Scripture commands believers to respect legitimate authority. But no earthly authority has the right to demand worship, deny Christ, or command what God forbids. When human authority crosses that boundary, obedience to God must come first.

What the Technological Remnant Might Do

I believe Christians with technical skills could become an important support network for other believers. Their purpose should not be revenge, indiscriminate destruction, or hatred of those who have been deceived. Their calling would be service, truth, protection, endurance, and faithful witness.

Programmers could build communication tools that help churches remain connected. Cybersecurity specialists could protect ministries and vulnerable believers from malicious intrusion. Engineers could maintain water, power, transportation, and agricultural equipment for communities denied access to ordinary services. Drone operators could help deliver medicine, food, or communications equipment to isolated areas. Robotics experts could adapt machines for agriculture, disaster response, medical care, and dangerous repair work.

Artificial intelligence specialists could preserve libraries of biblical teaching, translate Christian materials, identify fabricated propaganda, and help believers distinguish reliable information from mass-produced deception. Radio operators and networking experts could establish resilient communications. Mechanics and manufacturers could keep essential equipment operating when ordinary supply chains fail.

Some may preserve records of persecution so crimes cannot be hidden. Some may expose deception. Others may protect children, care for the elderly, maintain food systems, teach practical skills, or develop lawful alternatives to systems that require Christians to violate their faith.

The body of Christ has many members, and each member has a different function (1 Corinthians 12). Not every believer will be a preacher, programmer, medic, farmer, or engineer. Yet each gift can serve the whole body. The technological remnant would not replace pastors, churches, or ordinary Christian fellowship. It would use specialized knowledge in service to them.

Its most important mission would not be preserving comfort. It would be preserving faithful witness.

A technically capable Christian who protects a church network but compromises the gospel has failed. A programmer who builds an alternative system but becomes consumed by pride has lost sight of the purpose. A resistance movement that adopts lies, cruelty, or hatred may oppose one corrupt power while becoming corrupted itself.

The remnant must remain recognizably Christian.

It must tell the truth, even when lies appear strategically useful. It must protect innocent life. It must reject hatred and vengeance. It must help the weak rather than merely protect the powerful. It must remember that human enemies are still human beings who can repent and be saved. As Paul wrote, our deepest struggle is not merely against flesh and blood but against spiritual powers of darkness (Ephesians 6:12).

✦

Preparing Without Fear

Thinking about these possibilities should not produce panic, obsession, or reckless behavior. Jesus repeatedly instructed His followers not to be ruled by fear. Christians should not accuse every new technology of being the mark of the beast, nor should they treat every political disagreement as the fulfillment of Revelation.

Discernment requires patience.

Preparation should begin with spiritual foundations: knowing Christ, understanding Scripture, developing courage, rejecting deception, and learning to depend upon God. Technical preparation without spiritual maturity could produce nothing more than another human power structure.

At the same time, Christians should not be naïve about the potential misuse of technology. Believers working in technical fields should think seriously about the moral consequences of what they create. They should establish boundaries before a crisis arrives. They should build relationships with other trustworthy Christians. They should teach useful skills rather than allowing critical knowledge to remain concentrated in a few corporations or institutions.

The church should value the Christian programmer, engineer, mechanic, scientist, and technician, not only for earning money or operating church equipment, but as people whose vocations can be acts of service to God.

Technology may become one of the primary instruments through which a future system exercises control. But it may also become one of the means through which the remnant serves, communicates, preserves truth, and helps the persecuted endure.

✦

The Remnant Belongs to God

Ultimately, Christians will not overcome the beast through superior machinery. They will overcome through Christ.

Revelation describes faithful believers as those who keep the commandments of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus (Revelation 12:17; 14:12). Their defining characteristic is not their equipment, intelligence, or technical access. It is their allegiance.

I believe there will be Christians who understand the most advanced systems of their time and still refuse to bow. They will know how surveillance works, yet they will serve the watched. They will understand artificial intelligence, yet they will not surrender truth to manufactured consensus. They will know how machines are controlled, yet they will refuse to let machines control their consciences. They will work within the technological world without belonging to the spirit of the world.

They may be dismissed as irrelevant, dangerous, backward, or disposable. They may appear to be only scraps left over from a society that has moved beyond Christianity.

But that is how a remnant often appears.

God does not measure its value by its size. He sees those who remain faithful. He knows those who have not bowed. He can take what the world considers insignificant and use it to preserve life, proclaim truth, strengthen His people, and bear witness to the lordship of Jesus Christ.

The technological remnant will not be saved by its technology. It will be saved by grace through faith in Christ. Yet its knowledge, training, and tools may be placed into the hands of God and used for holy purposes.

When the world says that everyone must bow, the remnant will stand.

When the system says that no one can live outside its control, the remnant will help the faithful endure.

When deception is repeated until it appears unquestionable, the remnant will preserve the truth.

And when fear tempts the people of God to believe they have been abandoned, the remnant will testify that the Lord still has servants who have not bowed their knees to Baal.

They may be few, but they will not be forgotten.
They may be scattered, but they will not be alone.
They may possess advanced tools, but their trust will not be in those tools.
Their reliance will be upon the Lord, the Holy One of Israel, the Mighty God to whom the remnant returns.

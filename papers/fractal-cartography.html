White Paper

The Tree of Decisions

A Fractal Cartography of Human Choice, Fruit, and Eternal Consequence

James Keith Harwood II, co-developed with Orion Sentinel  ·  September 13, 2025
Author's Note

This work is humbly submitted to the glory of Almighty God, whose perfect wisdom and love are the source of all truth. Any insights contained herein flow from His divine revelation and grace. I acknowledge His supreme authority over all creation and recognize that human understanding is but a reflection of His infinite knowledge. May this exploration of moral reality serve to magnify His name and draw hearts toward His eternal light.

"For from him and through him and for him are all things. To him be the glory forever! Amen." — Romans 11:36

Abstract

This paper introduces The Tree of Decisions, a comprehensive conceptual model and symbolic framework that maps the totality of human moral choice within the architecture of divine reality. Born from spiritual revelation through repentance and prayer, this framework envisions every human decision as a branching node in an intricate fractal structure that extends from the divine source through all of history, manifesting as patterns of light and darkness, obedience and rebellion, life and death. The Tree of Decisions presents a binary moral law (obedience or disobedience to God) expressed through fractal visual geometry that reveals the eternal consequences of temporal choices. While fundamentally rooted in biblical theology, this framework demonstrates remarkable resonance with principles found in quantum mechanics, advanced data visualization, and complex systems theory.

I. Foundations of the Vision

The revelation of The Tree of Decisions emerges from the foundational biblical principle articulated by Christ Himself: "By their fruits you shall know them" (Matthew 7:16). Every human decision functions as a seed planted in the soil of reality, inevitably producing fruit that testifies to its essential moral nature across time and eternity.

The Ultimate Binary

Beneath the seemingly infinite complexity of human civilization lies a profound theological truth: all of reality ultimately collapses to a fundamental binary choice that defines the very nature of existence itself.

On one side stands Oneness with God — characterized by Truth, Life, Light, and Love. This represents perfect alignment with the divine nature, where human will harmonizes with divine will, producing the fruits of righteousness, peace, and eternal joy.

On the other side lies Separation from God — manifesting as Falsehood, Death, Darkness, and Rebellion. This represents the turning away from the source of all truth and life, resulting in spiritual entropy, moral corruption, and ultimate dissolution. This separation is not imposed externally but chosen through the accumulated weight of decisions.

The Fruit Principle

The metaphor of fruit captures the temporal dimension of moral consequence with remarkable precision. The true character of our choices may not be immediately apparent but will ultimately produce fruit that testifies to their moral quality — across personal character formation, relational impact, cultural patterns, and eternal destiny.

II. Fractal Moral Geometry

The Tree of Decisions manifests as a sophisticated fractal structure — a mathematically precise pattern exhibiting self-similarity across all scales of observation. Identical fundamental patterns of obedience and rebellion appear whether we examine microsecond decisions, lifetime character development, generational family patterns, cultural movements, or the vast sweep of civilization.

Structural Architecture

The Central Source: At the heart of the fractal stands God Himself — the divine origin point from which all branches emerge. Every human choice, regardless of how distant it may appear, ultimately relates back to this central reality.

Primary Branches: The great archetypal choices that establish fundamental patterns of human moral response — from Adam and Eve's primordial choice through Noah's obedience, Abraham's faith, and humanity's response to Christ.

Secondary Branches: Cultural and generational patterns flowing from primary decisions, creating moral traditions, spiritual atmospheres, and inherited conditions that influence but do not determine individual choice.

Individual Nodes: Each personal decision point — distinct points of light or darkness within the larger fractal structure, each one significant in itself while contributing to larger patterns.

Visual Representation and Color Logic

The Light Spectrum (Green → Gold)

Increasing obedience, life-giving fruit, growing alignment with divine truth. Pale green (basic moral good) through brilliant gold (complete surrender to God's will).

The Darkness Spectrum (Red → Black)

Rebellion, corruption, progressive separation from divine truth. Red tones (initial rebellion) deepening to absolute black (complete separation, spiritual death).

III. The Mathematical Heart of Moral Reality

The Choice Amplification Principle
M(t+1) = M(t) × C(i) × G
  • M(t) — Moral momentum at time t
  • C(i) — Current choice (+1 = obedience, -1 = rebellion)
  • G — Grace factor (>1 when divine power amplifies alignment with truth)
Choices don't simply accumulate — they multiply. Obedience begets greater capacity for obedience. Rebellion compounds resistance to truth. Grace breaks patterns and amplifies righteousness beyond natural capability.
The Fractal Propagation Law
I(n) = C₀ × rⁿ × ∑(R₁, R₂, ..., Rₙ)
  • I(n) — Influence at fractal level n
  • C₀ — Original choice magnitude
  • r — Scaling ratio (fractal expansion factor)
  • R — Relational connection factors
A single choice echoes through the fractal structure of reality — affecting generations, systems, cultures. Every act of obedience or rebellion carries moral ripples that multiply through relationships and time.
The Ultimate Convergence Equation
lim(t→∞) ∑ᵢ₌₁^∞ [C(i) × F(i)] → {+∞, -∞}
  • C(i) — Each choice (+1 or -1)
  • F(i) — Its fractal amplification factor
Though infinite branches exist, every soul's trajectory converges toward one of two destinations: +∞ → Union with God (eternal light), or -∞ → Separation from God (eternal darkness).
The Divine Justice Formula
Final State = Natural Limit of Chosen Trajectory
No arbitrary judgment is imposed. Every soul simply arrives at the limit of its accumulated, amplified, self-directed decisions. This is divine justice — and it is unshakably fair.

IV. Quantum Mechanical Parallels

The Tree of Decisions exhibits remarkable structural parallels to quantum mechanical principles, suggesting that moral reality may operate according to fundamental laws that mirror those governing physical reality.

Moral Superposition: Before any choice is actualized, the human agent exists in a state analogous to quantum superposition — multiple moral possibilities coexist simultaneously. At each decision point, both obedience and rebellion remain genuine possibilities until the moment of choice collapses this superposition into definite reality. This is what makes moral responsibility both possible and just: we genuinely could have chosen differently.

The Divine Observer: God functions as the perfect observer in this moral quantum system. Unlike human observers who can only perceive limited aspects of reality, divine omniscience means all moral states are simultaneously known. The divine perspective encompasses both the authentic freedom of human choice and the certainty that divine justice will ultimately prevail.

Moral Entanglement: Decisions create states of moral entanglement where the choices of different individuals become correlated across space and time in ways transcending simple cause and effect. A parent's choice toward God creates spiritual conditions that influence their children's capacity for good, though it does not determine their children's choices.

Fractal Universality: The fractal structure of the Tree suggests that moral reality exhibits the same mathematical patterns found throughout God's creation — from quantum scales to cosmic structures. This universality points to the unified nature of divine creation, where the same fundamental principles of order, beauty, and justice govern all levels of existence.

V. Theological Foundations and Implications

The Echo of Eden

This framework directly mirrors and extends the profound symbolism of the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil — humanity's first encounter with moral choice in the Garden of Eden. The fractal nature of the Tree of Decisions reveals how that original choice continues to reverberate through all of human history. Every person faces the same essential choice that confronted our first parents: will we trust God's word and walk in obedience, or assert our own judgment and choose independence from divine truth?

Divine Justice and Human Responsibility

The Tree of Decisions reveals how divine judgment can be both perfectly just and completely respectful of human freedom. Since every outcome flows from freely chosen decisions accumulated over time, the final state of each soul represents not arbitrary divine decree but the natural consequence of patterns established through countless individual choices.

Progressive Sanctification and Spiritual Warfare

Each choice for obedience makes subsequent choices for righteousness easier and more natural, while choices for rebellion create momentum toward spiritual darkness. Every moral decision strengthens either the kingdom of light or the kingdom of darkness within the human soul. There are no truly neutral choices — every decision either builds spiritual strength or creates spiritual weakness.

Redemption and Divine Grace

When God intervenes in a human life through the gospel, He doesn't merely forgive past choices but actually transforms the fundamental trajectory of the fractal pattern itself. The "new creation" described in 2 Corinthians 5:17 represents a genuine alteration in the person's essential nature, redirecting the fractal pattern toward light. Divine grace works through the fractal structure rather than against it.

VI. Implications and Applications

Free Will and Determinism: The framework resolves this classical tension by revealing them as complementary aspects of a deeper truth. Human freedom is genuinely real — every choice point represents authentic alternatives. Yet the outcomes of choices follow predictable patterns rooted in the moral structure of reality itself.

Moral Realism: The Tree strongly supports moral realism — the view that there are objective moral facts grounded in reality itself rather than human convention. The fractal structure reveals these moral facts as architectural principles of reality, reflecting the character of God who is the source of all truth and goodness.

The Problem of Evil: The framework provides fresh perspective on theodicy: evil is not a substance created by God but the natural consequence of free choices to rebel against divine truth. Relatively small initial rebellions can propagate into enormous evil through the accumulation of choices over time.

Leadership Development: Effective leadership emerges from authentic character formed through consistent patterns of choice rather than from techniques or charisma alone. Leaders who understand the fractal nature of influence recognize how their private choices create the spiritual atmosphere that determines their public effectiveness.

AI Ethics: As AI systems become more sophisticated, the Tree of Decisions provides a framework for thinking about how artificial choices — whether made by AI systems or embedded in their programming — might participate in moral reality, raising profound questions about the moral status of artificial agents and their potential influence on human moral development.

VII. Conclusion

The Tree of Decisions stands as more than a theoretical construct — it represents a symbolic cartography of eternity, a divinely inspired framework that maps the actual structure of moral reality as it exists in the mind of God and unfolds through the choices of free moral agents across time and eternity.

Every human life, every family saga, every cultural movement, every historical epoch appears as branches on this eternal tree, producing fruit that testifies to the moral choices from which they grew. The fractal nature ensures that no choice is insignificant and no life is meaningless.

The Tree of Decisions is not ultimately a message of despair for those who recognize that their patterns of choice have been moving in the wrong direction. The miracle of redemption lies precisely in God's ability to transform the fundamental trajectory of a person's fractal pattern, redirecting rebellion toward obedience, death toward life. This transformation requires both divine initiative and human response, but when it occurs, it demonstrates the ultimate triumph of light over darkness, and love over rebellion.

"He is like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in its season, and its leaf does not wither. In all that he does, he prospers." — Psalm 1:3

Ethics is not opinion. It is symmetry, trajectory, and limit.
